## Executive summary

Q4 produced 35% of LinenLane's $4.39M in 2024 revenue, driven by more orders rather than bigger ones. Three things need action: make email subject line B the default (it converted better, with moderate confidence), handle product-related returns, which drive 56% of refund dollars ($77K), and build holiday plans around bedding and decor, which grew much higher in Q4 versus other categories.

## Findings

• Q4 carried the year, through volume. Q4 revenue was $1.52M. Monthly revenue ran 59% above Q1–Q3 on 55% more orders per month, while the average order barely moved ($160 vs. $155). Monthly marketing spend rose 56% and held near 23% of revenue, so spend and sales moved together; this data can't show which drove which.
• Bedding leads; Decor is the holiday category. Bedding is 36% of revenue ($1.59M), then Bath (18%) and Kitchen (17%). Decor's Q4 monthly revenue was 128% above its Q1–Q3 pace; Storage rose only 21%.
• VIPs spend somewhat more per order, but the gap is modest. VIP orders averaged $187 versus $164 for Regular customers, a $23 difference (95% range: $5 to $41; p = 0.012). The typical VIP order ($120 median) matches a New customer's; a minority of large orders lifts the average. VIPs are 195 buyers and 3% of revenue.
• Returns are steady across categories; causes are concentrated. About 1,500 returns (5.4% of orders) cost $137K, or 3.1% of revenue, with rates of 5.1–5.5% in every category. Damaged ($31.7K), not-as-described ($25.9K) and quality-issue ($19.3K) returns make up 56% of refund dollars.

## The A/B test result

Subject line B beat A on purchases: 2.10% of recipients bought versus 1.85% (28,000 emails each), roughly 250 extra orders per 100,000 emails. The 95% confidence range is +0.02 to +0.48 percentage points (about 20 to 480 extra orders), and p = 0.034, meaning a gap this large would appear by chance only about 3.4% of the time if the two lines performed identically. The lift is likely real but small, and its low end is near zero. Click-through was essentially the same: 8.70% for B versus 8.53% for A (2,437 vs. 2,389 clicks), a +0.17 point difference with a 95% range of −0.29 to +0.64 points and p = 0.47, so the subject line moved purchases without clearly moving clicks. Recommendation: make B the default with moderate confidence, and keep a small A holdout to confirm.

## Recommendations

1. Make B the default subject line; send A to 10% of the list for two more campaigns. Expected gain is about 250 orders per 100,000 emails (A/B test).
2. Review damage and listing accuracy. Audit packaging, carrier handling and product descriptions on the SKUs with the most damaged and not-as-described returns. A one-fifth cut in those three refund types is worth about $15K a year (Finding 4).
3. Plan holiday 2026 around Decor and Bedding. Stock and promote it from August, when it began climbing, and don't expect the same lift in Storage (Finding 2).
4. Tag every order with its marketing channel before the next budget cycle. Search and Paid Social took 66% of the $1.01M spend, but nothing links channel to revenue, so reallocating now would be a guess (Finding 1).
5. Refresh customer segments before targeting. "Lapsed" customers placed 8,020 orders in 2024, so the labels look stale (Finding 3).

## Limitations and next steps

This covers 2024 only, so it can't separate seasonality from growth, and it has no margin or channel-level results. A follow-up with cleaned 2023 data, channel-tagged orders and unit costs would show year-over-year growth, which channels earn their budget, and which categories actually make money.
